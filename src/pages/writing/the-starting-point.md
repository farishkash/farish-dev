If we don't start our journey, we will never reach our destination.

I am one of those who believe developers should have a blog or portfolio. Having a portfolio shares your work; having a blog shares your thoughts.

Sometimes potential recruiters like to see *both*.

A lot of the work I do isn't in the public view, so besides making some personal projects to have something to show, I decided sharing my thoughts was the quickest route to get up and running.

That *MVP* that I talk about in my class.

Now I had a site, but back in early 2019, I took it down. I felt it was dated. I truly believed a new version would be up within a month. Sometimes life just gets in the way of your plans.

I even mention in one of the videos I made for [Codecademy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64SkvEu0vNo) that taking down my old site was a mistake.

As many of us do, when we feel something isn't a priority, we find ways to put it off. Eventually, I told myself it would be up by the end of 2019. Then I started working for Trilogy and that turned into another delay.

Then 2020 strikes. To be fair, I think 2020 as a whole is a good excuse for anything that didn't go as planned.

Now 2021 rolls around and I have a few goals. I always believe we should have *personal learning goals*. My learning focus for that year was Next.js, TypeScript, and Python while preparing for a Master's in Data Science.

Next.js powered the original version of this blog.

I had a lot of thoughts to share: EdTech, work/life balance, code, and whatever came next. Hopefully those of you who stumble across the site find something useful or interesting.

As always, thanks for reading.
